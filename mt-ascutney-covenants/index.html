The 1984 Mt. Ascutney Covenants

The ski resort closed in 2010. Its covenants still govern our homes.

The Mt. Ascutney protective covenants are 26 pages of rules the resort company recorded in 1984 on every lot and condo at the base of the mountain in West Windsor, Vermont. The resort is gone and the town owns the mountain, but these rules still decide what we can build, cut, and keep on our own land.

They were written for a resort that's gone

In 1984 the resort company rewrote the rules to support a big expansion. Here's what the rules assumed, next to what's true now.

The purpose

1984A hotel, condo, and health-club expansion at the ski resort.

TodayThe resort closed for good in 2010. In 2015 town voters bought the mountain, 254 to 79, and the land is conserved forever.

Who approves your plans

1984Mt. Ascutney Corp., the company running the resort.

TodayMFW Associates, an investment firm that took over the resort in foreclosure.

Sewer and roads

1984Hook up to the resort's sewer. Pay the developer road fees "including therein a reasonable profit."

TodayThe town runs the sewer and water systems and maintains the roads.

Skiing

1984Owners get ski access at regular rates.

TodayAscutney Outdoors, a volunteer nonprofit, runs the mountain under a town lease.

They still decide everyday things on your land

Wherever the rules say "Ascutney," they mean the resort company or whoever inherited its rights. Today that's MFW.

  • Cutting a tree

    A pine about as thick as a broom handle needs written permission to cut, even if it's dead and leaning toward your roof.

    …may not be cut for any purpose whatsoever without the express prior written approval of AscutneyArticle IV.C.10

    Without the rules, you'd use your own judgment.

  • Building a shed, deck, or sign

    Plans need private written approval, on top of town permits.

    No building, sign, facility or structure shall be constructed… except in accord with plans and specifications which have been approved in writing by Ascutney…Article V.D

    Without the rules, you'd need town and state permits only, like everywhere else in West Windsor.

  • Keeping chickens

    Banned outright, however quiet or well kept.

    No livestock or poultry of any kind shall be raised, bred or kept on any lotArticle V.E

    Without the rules, the town's poultry ordinance would apply, and it already handles noise.

  • Selling your home

    When you accept an offer, you must send the terms to MFW, which has 10 days to buy your home at that price instead. In a 2022 sale here, the seller paid MFW a "waiver review fee."

    Such written notice shall constitute an offer to sell or lease the same premises to Ascutney upon the same termsArticle VII(b)

    Without the rules, it's a normal sale.

  • Paying dues

    The rules require dues to an owners' association. The town already maintains the roads, and owners aren't sent a budget showing what dues pay for.

    Without the rules, the association would stay, and dues would be optional, more like a donation.

  • Being sued by a neighbor

    Any owner can sue another over a technical violation, even one everyone has ignored for years.

    Without the rules, real problems go through the town, with a hearing and a public record.

Everything that protects the neighborhood stays

  • Town zoning
  • The town's noise and poultry ordinances
  • Building and septic permits
  • Act 250, Vermont's land use law
  • Town-maintained roads, sewer, and water
  • The owners' association, as a voluntary group

When the association took on a neighborhood problem in 2025, it used a town ordinance, not the covenants.

They may not even be valid

Reading the full document and the public record turns up real doubts.

  • Already cancelled

    Some rules are already overridden by law

    Federal law overrides the limits on satellite dishes. Vermont law says no covenant can stop solar panels or clotheslines.

  • Unconfirmed

    They came after many lots were sold

    Vermont's Supreme Court has said neighborhood-wide rules must be in place before the first lot sells, or agreed to by every owner. Sky Hawk lots were sold about 15 years before 1984, though their original covenants let the company make changes.

  • Unconfirmed

    The rules admit nobody was sure what land they covered

    They also needed a signed certification to take effect. It may have been recorded, but it isn't in the copy that comes with closing documents.

Questions neighbors ask

Could a neighbor build something awful?

The same town zoning and permits that apply everywhere else in West Windsor still apply here.

Would this shut down the association?

No. It's a separate nonprofit, and only its own members can dissolve it. Its seats on the Ascutney Outdoors board and the town's utility committee, and its July 4th events, can all continue.

Why not just fix the bad parts?

Owners can't. Only the developer's successor can propose changes, and today that's MFW.

What happens next?

At the association's October 2026 meeting, the roughly 30 owners there agreed to start looking into what it would take to end the covenants. Ending them takes a vote of a majority of lot owners, so it needs support from neighbors well beyond that room. The next automatic renewal is in May 2029. Sign up for updates

Does this apply to me?

Probably, if you own in Sky Hawk Village, Mountain Village, or the Ascutney Mountainside or Mountain Village condominiums. Your deed or title insurance will mention "protective covenants" from Mt. Ascutney Ski Area, Inc. or Mt. Ascutney Corp.
